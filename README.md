## What an OKX promo code actually is

A referral code links a new account to a promotional campaign. It is not a coupon that shaves a fixed amount off a purchase, and it is not a package with a defined price. OKX attaches different campaigns to different codes, regions and time windows, which is why the same exchange can hand one person €400 in milestone vouchers and another person a percentage off trading fees.

That structure explains most of the confusion in the search results. Sites publish codes with wildly different promises, from "up to $10,000" to "50% off fees", and all of them may be describing a real campaign that existed for a specific group of users at a specific moment.

## Five things to know before you type a code

**1. You cannot add a code after registration.** There is no field in settings to add one later, and support will not backdate it. As one guide puts it plainly: "You can't add it later, which is why questions like how to change the OKX referral code don't have a solution." OKX's own referral FAQ confirms the code is prefilled or entered at sign-up, and rewards are tied to that enrolment.

**2. Sub-accounts are excluded.** OKX's referral terms state that "sub-accounts will not be eligible to participate in this referral program, receive any rewards, or be invited as invitees." If you register first and create sub-accounts afterwards, the sub-accounts still earn nothing.

**3. KYC is mandatory, and it has two levels.** Basic verification asks for your full name, date of birth, country of residence and a government-issued ID number. Advanced verification adds a photo of the ID document plus a selfie. The first voucher tier is unlocked by verification itself, so skipping it costs you the easiest €50 in the ladder.

**4. Everything is task-based.** Deposits, trading volume and verification are the triggers. There is no sign-up credit, no welcome balance sitting in the account waiting to be used, and no reward for merely creating a username.

**5. Trades funded by other OKX rewards usually do not count.** This is the detail most code pages leave out, and it answers the question "why did my volume not unlock the next tier?" OKX's own material notes that trades made with accumulated rewards and perks, including rebate cards, contract vouchers, USDC fee waivers and welcome bonuses, typically do not count as valid trades for campaign tasks. If you fund a position with a voucher and hit €10,000 of volume, the milestone may stay grey.

There are also time limits. OKX's referral terms describe trading tasks measured within 14 days of sign-up on some programs and 30 days on others, with the referral page stating which applies. Rewards must be claimed within the stated event period, claimable through the Rewards Center in the app, and at least one regional entity restricts rewards from trading or withdrawal for up to 90 days after they are granted. Read the terms attached to your own region before assuming a voucher is immediately spendable.

## Is the $10,000 OKX sign-up bonus real?

You will see "up to $10,000 USDT" attached to almost every OKX code page, including ones published this month. The figure is an aggregate ceiling across many separate campaigns, not a single payment.

Here is what gets added together to reach it: task-based trading rewards, a welcome bonus worth up to $50 USDT for simple tasks, mystery boxes containing up to $50 in USDT or BTC, limited-time event prizes, and fee rebate credits. The same pages that print the $10,000 figure also state that payouts depend on your activity and that you need to deposit and meet trading thresholds to reach the higher amounts. ## Why your code didn't work: a checklist

Work through these in order. In most cases one of the first four explains it.

1. **The code was not entered during registration.** Nothing can fix this on the same account.
2. **KYC was never completed.** Unverified accounts do not unlock voucher tiers.
3. **No qualifying deposit was made.** The deposit tier needs €100 or more in the EU program, and funds moved internally may not count.
4. **The code was wrong, expired, or region-locked.** Codes are frequently tied to a country.
5. **You did not click "Join now" on the campaign page.** This one catches people who did everything else right. Campaign-level promotions, including deposit bonuses, require manual enrolment on the campaign landing page. Third-party tracking of OKX campaign pages found that skipping the button means "the discount is not activated, even if the promotion is publicly live and the user is logged in." OKX's own campaign copy uses the same language: "Join the campaign and deposit USDT."
6. **You traded with reward funds.** See point five in the previous section.
7. **You are on a sub-account, or the invite crossed regions.** OKX's terms exclude cross-region invitations from referral rewards in some cases.

## Do third-party codes actually work?

Sometimes, briefly. That is the honest answer, and one coupon site says so directly: "A code that works at breakfast can be dead by lunch. Nobody can promise otherwise, so we don't stamp anything here as 'working' or 'verified'. We tell you the chance of a code working instead."

The same tracker's data gives a sense of the churn: codes were available in 19 of 48 tracked weeks, roughly 11 new codes appeared in a 30-day window, and typical code lifespan ran about 30 days. User voting on another coupon site shows the same pattern, with one code reported as working and not working by different users on the same day in early November 2026.

There is a second warning sign. Multiple sites each declare their own code to be "the best OKX referral code", using different codes. At most one of those claims can be true, and probably none of them are, since the reward you receive depends on the campaign your account is placed into rather than on the string of characters.

## When OKX runs its bigger promotions

Third-party tracking of OKX's promotional calendar identifies a few recurring windows. These come from coupon-site analysis, not from OKX announcements, so treat them as patterns rather than promises.

The heaviest activity falls in October through December, with a secondary peak from April to June. September and October carry an autumn sale on trading fees in some years. December brings an Advent Calendar format that releases BTC rewards through daily product-specific tasks, and missing a day forfeits that day's allocation with no retroactive credit. The launchpad's IEO windows historically cluster in January to March and July to September.

If you are going to trade a large volume anyway, timing it inside one of these windows gives you a better chance of a live campaign existing alongside your code.

## Which code should you use?

Pick a code from a source you have reason to trust, enter it in the referral field during registration, and then judge the offer by the tier table your own account displays. The code string matters less than the campaign it attaches to, and campaign values change monthly.

Three reader situations, three different answers:

- **Testing the waters.** Complete KYC and make a modest deposit. That captures the first two tiers without committing trading volume. Do not chase the €10,000 and €50,000 milestones for the sake of a voucher.
- **Planning to trade regularly.** The fee rebate attached to your account matters more than a one-time voucher ladder. Check what the Rewards Center shows after registration, and check whether the rebate stacks with your volume tier.

Before you deposit anything, open the referral page and read the tier list your region shows, confirm the reward currency and product, and check the claim window. The vouchers are real, but they are credits with conditions attached, and the conditions are where most people lose them.
